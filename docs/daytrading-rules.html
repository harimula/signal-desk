Day trading rules: stocks, CALLs and PUTs

The professional rulebook the bot follows when Day-trade is switched on in Bot settings. Every rule has a reason, and under each one is how the bot applies it. The bot never shorts stock: a short setup is traded with a PUT or not at all.

Kept in step with the engine (bot/daytrade.mjs) on every change · last updated 2026-10-22

Experimental. Day trading was not part of the 33-year research that selected the RSI-2 swing rule. These rules are the industry's standard discipline, not a proven edge: paper-trade them for weeks, read the Performance table, and keep the size small. Not advice.

The non-negotiablesthe rules that keep an account alive; the setups come second

N1 Know the stop before you enter.

No stop price, no trade. The stop goes where the setup is proven wrong (the other side of the range, below the pullback low), not at a round loss you can tolerate.

Bot: every entry carries its stop, target and entry price inside the order id; without a valid stop daySetup returns nothing.
N2 Risk a fixed small slice of the account per trade: 0.5 %, never above 1 %.

Ten losses in a row at 0.5 % is −5 %: survivable. At 5 % per trade it is −40 %.

Bot: Risk per day trade (default 0.5 %) sizes every trade so the stop loses at most that; the stock / CALL / PUT money limits cap it further.
N3 Only take trades that pay at least twice the risk.

With a 2R target you can be right only 40 % of the time and still make money. 1:1 trades need a 55 %+ hit rate, which almost nobody has.

Bot: Target default 2R; the setup is discarded if the risk is under 0.2 % or over 2.5 % of the price (too tight to survive noise, or too wide to size).
N4 A daily loss limit ends the day. Full stop.

The worst losses come from trying to win it back. 2–3 % down on the day: close the screen.

Bot: the account-wide Daily loss limit (3 %) blocks new day trades and closes options; Losses in a row that end the day (2) stops after two consecutive day-trade losses.
N5 Few trades. A handful of A-setups beats twenty B-setups.

Each trade costs spread and slippage; over-trading is the most common way accounts bleed.

Bot: Max day trades per day (3), one per stock per day, no re-entry after a stop.
N6 A day trade ends the same day. Never "let it become an investment".

Holding a loser overnight converts a planned small loss into an unplanned gap risk.

Bot: everything day-traded is sold at 3:50 PM ET (options 3:45) whatever the price.
N7 Never move a stop away from the price; never add to a loser.
Bot: stops only move toward the price (to breakeven at +1R); there is no averaging-down code path.

1 · What to tradeliquidity first

1.1 Liquid, large names only: price ≥ $10 (better $20), average volume ≥ 1 million shares a day, a spread of a few cents. Illiquid stocks make the stop a suggestion.
Bot: the universe is your list or the market scan (top N by dollar volume, minimum price); day trades inherit it. For day trading set the scan minimum price to $20+.
1.2 Only stocks that are "in play" today: relative volume ≥ 1.2–1.5× a normal day by this time. Quiet stocks drift; busy stocks trend.
Bot: Minimum relative volume (1.2) against the stock's 10-day average, weighted for the time of day.
1.3 Skip stocks with their own news today (earnings, FDA, guidance, halts): the levels do not hold and options are priced for a jump.
Bot: the breaking-news guard's per-stock check applies to day entries.
1.4 No penny stocks, no low-float runners, no stocks you cannot borrow or that are halted.
Bot: never shorts stock (short setups become PUTs), so borrow and halt risk on shorts do not arise; the price floor removes penny stocks.

2 · When to tradethe clock is a filter

2.1 Do not trade the first 15 minutes. The open is where the opening range is built, spreads are widest, and reversals are violent. Watch; do not act.
Bot: Entries from 9:45 (the 15-minute opening range must be complete).
2.2 Best hours: 9:45–11:30 and 2:00–3:30 ET. Lunch (11:30–1:30) is chop: fewer setups qualify because volume dries up.
Bot: entries allowed 9:45–15:30 by default; the relative-volume rule naturally thins out lunchtime trades. Narrow the window in settings if you want the morning only.
2.3 No new trades in the last 30 minutes; flat before the closing auction.
Bot: no entries after until (15:30); flat at 15:50, options at 15:45.
2.4 Stand aside around scheduled shocks: Fed decisions (2:00 PM), CPI and jobs reports (8:30 AM — the open after them is wild), Fed minutes.
Bot: the economic-calendar guard pauses all entries around these; options are closed before a Fed decision.

3 · Market filtertrade with the tide

3.1 Longs only when the market is up on the day, shorts only when it is down. The simplest definition: SPY above its VWAP = up, below = down. Most stocks move with the index; fighting it halves your odds.
Bot: marketMode() from SPY's 5-minute VWAP, re-checked every cycle. Within ±0.05 % of VWAP the market is "undecided" and nothing is entered.
3.2 Breaking news pauses trading. A war, policy or crisis headline means the levels you planned around no longer apply.
Bot: the breaking-news guard pauses day entries for 30 minutes; open day trades keep their stops and targets.

4 · The two setupsmechanical, no discretion

Setup A — Opening-range breakout (ORB)

A1 Define the range: the high and low of the first 15 minutes (9:30–9:45).
A2 Long entry: a 5-minute bar closes above the range high, the previous bar had not, price is above VWAP, relative volume qualifies, and the market filter says up. The entry is the current price (limit +0.1 %). Short is the exact mirror (close below the range low, below VWAP, market down) and is taken as a PUT.
A3 Stop: just under the range low (0.1 ATR below it), but never more than 1.5 ATR from the entry: if the range is huge, the stop tightens to 1.5 ATR. Target: 2R.
Bot: stop = max(rangeLow − 0.1·ATR, entry − 1.5·ATR); ATR is the 14-bar average true range on 5-minute bars.
A4 Only the first breakout counts. A second attempt after a failed breakout is a different, weaker trade — skipped.
Bot: "fresh" means the prior bar had not crossed; one trade per stock per day.

Setup B — VWAP pullback on a trend day

B1 Trend day: at least 70 % of the session's 5-minute closes have been on one side of VWAP (as VWAP stood at each bar).
B2 Pullback and reclaim: the previous bar touched VWAP (within 0.15 %), and the current bar closed back on the trend side and above the pullback bar's high (longs). Mirror for shorts → PUT.
B3 Stop: 0.25 ATR beyond the pullback low (high for shorts) or VWAP, whichever is further. Target: 2R.
B4 What is deliberately missing: no counter-trend fades, no scalping, no news plays, no trading off Level 2 or the tape. Those need a human's judgment and reaction time, and are where most retail day traders lose.

5 · Position sizerisk decides the size, not conviction

5.1 Shares = (account × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop). Then cap by the money allowed per trade.
$100,000 account, 0.5 % risk = $500. Entry $101.60, stop $100.40 → $1.20 per share → 416 shares. Capital cap 10 % = $10,000 → 98 shares. The bot buys 98.
Bot: dayShares(); the cap is the stock sleeve's per trade money in Capital allocation.
5.2 Options are sized on the option's own stop: contracts = risk $ ÷ (premium × 100 × option-stop %). A $2.05 contract with a 40 % stop risks $82 per contract → 6 contracts for $500 of risk, then the CALL/PUT money cap applies.

6 · Managing the tradedecide once, then obey

6.1 Stop hit → out at market, immediately. No "wait for the close of the bar".
Bot: checks the live price every beat (about every 2 seconds); any resting take-profit order is cancelled first so the market sell can fill.
6.2 Target as a resting limit order so it fills at exchange speed, not when a human notices.
Bot: a limit sell sits at the target from the moment the entry fills.
6.3 At +1R move the stop to breakeven. A trade that went your way should not turn into a loss.
6.4 Time stop: if the trade has not made at least 0.3R after 60 minutes, it is not working — take the small result and free the capital.
Bot: Time stop (60 min, 0.3R).
6.5 An entry that does not fill within minutes has missed its moment.
Bot: unfilled day-trade buys are cancelled after 10 minutes.
6.6 Flat by 3:50 PM (options 3:45), no exceptions; no re-entry after a stop.

7 · CALLs and PUTs intradaysame setups, different instrument

7.1 Buy options only. Never sell (write) them intraday — unlimited risk has no place in a rulebook like this.
7.2 Never same-day expiry (0DTE). Theta eats the premium by the hour and gamma makes the P/L a coin flip. Use the nearest expiry at least one trading day out, at most about a week.
Bot: Option expiry, max days out (7); the earliest expiry considered is tomorrow.
7.3 At-the-money strike (delta about 0.50). It moves roughly half a dollar per dollar of the stock, with the least time-decay drag per unit of movement for a one-day hold.
Bot: the strike nearest the current price.
7.4 Spread discipline: the bid-ask spread must be under 10 % of the ask (professionals want under 5 %). A wide spread is a loss you pay twice.
Bot: contracts with a spread over 10 % are skipped; the buy limit is the mid plus 2 %, never above the ask.
7.5 Exits on the option: take profit at +50 %, stop at −40 %, and exit when the stock hits the trade's stop or target or the time stop fires. Whichever comes first.
Bot: Option take-profit / Option stop, plus the stock-level rules carried in the order id.
7.6 Avoid IV traps: no options on a stock with earnings today or tomorrow (implied volatility collapses after the event), none held into a Fed decision.
Bot: news guard skips stocks with fresh news/earnings; options are closed before a Fed decision; everything is flat by 3:45 anyway.
7.7 Keep the option sleeve small until it has 50+ trades of history: a fixed-dollar per-trade and total cap in Capital allocation does that.

8 · Daily limits and the pattern-day-trader rulethe rules outside your control

8.1 Pattern-day-trader (FINRA): an account under $25,000 that makes four or more day trades in five business days is restricted. Three per rolling week is the practical limit for small accounts.
Bot: no new day trades once the broker reports 3 day trades in 5 days on an account under $25,000. The paper account is $100,000, so this does not bind there.
8.2 Weekly loss limit (6 %) and the losing-streak rule apply to day trades as to everything else.
8.3 Costs are real: commissions may be zero but spread and slippage are not; the 10-minute fill cancel and the 10 % option-spread rule exist to keep them bounded.

9 · Reviewwhat to expect, how to judge it

9.1 Judge after 50+ trades, not 5. With 2R targets a 40–50 % win rate is normal and profitable; a run of six losses in a row is statistically ordinary.
Bot: the Performance table on the Bot trades tab splits Day trades (same day) from Swing and shows expectancy and profit factor per group.
9.2 One change at a time. If you tweak the target and the volume filter together you learn nothing.
9.3 Keep the proven rule running at full size (RSI-2 swing) and run day trading small beside it until it earns more.

Evidencewhat the data says, and its limits

Intraday history is short. No public feed has 5-minute bars from 30 or 40 years ago; the broker's IEX feed starts in 2016. So this rulebook is tested on roughly ten years of 5-minute bars, every bar, for the bot's universe plus SPY, using the same code the live engine runs. The swing rule (RSI-2) is the one with 33–40 years of daily data behind it.

Where to read it: Bot trades tab → “🧪 Day-trading test”. It shows the verdict, the bot's defaults next to the best-tested combination, what happens without the market filter or with one setup only, and every year's result. The study refreshes on the first of each month and whenever it is re-run from GitHub Actions (day-trading-research).

How to read it: expectancy per trade in R (profit as a multiple of the risk) and in dollars at $500 risk; profit factor above 1.1 and most years positive is the bar for calling it an edge. Shorts are simulated as stock shorts, a stand-in for PUTs that ignores the option's spread and time decay — the real PUT result will be somewhat worse.

Using it: Bot settings → 4b → “Put these values in the form”, then Save. Judge the live paper results after 50+ trades before trusting either the test or the defaults.
