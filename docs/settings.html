What every bot setting does — with a worked example

One entry per control on the Bot settings tab: what it changes, the default, your current value, the allowed range, and a concrete example in dollars so you can see what the number actually does. Defaults marked tested are the values the 33-year research used. experimental parts were never tested on history.

Every field in Bot settings also explains itself: hover a label (tap it on a phone). · Kept in step with the settings tab on every change · last updated 2026-10-25 (second audit; SPY money per trade; tickers checked before saving)

How settings workwhere they live and when they take effect

Settings are saved to the file bot/config.json in your GitHub repo. The bot re-reads that file once a minute while it runs, so most changes take effect within about a minute — no restart needed. Each setting has an allowed range (shown below); since 2026-10-24 the page refuses to save a value outside it and tells you which one, so what you see on the page is always what the bot runs. The sticky bar at the top shows unsaved changes until you press Save settings to the bot, then “Saved” with the time, or the exact error if GitHub refused.

Two kinds of values: a percentage of your money (so they scale if your stake changes) or fixed dollars (section 3 lets you choose per instrument). Where this page says “money” it means your stake if you set one in section 3, otherwise the whole practice account.

The running exampleused in every entry below

To keep the numbers real, every example on this page uses your current settings on the $100,000 practice account (no stake set) and a made-up stock, ACME, in a long uptrend at $100, well above its 200-day average of $90.

Your money is split stocks 70% ($70,000) · CALLs 20% ($20,000) · PUTs 5% ($5,000) · SPY quick options 5% ($5,000). Risk per stock trade 1%, 12% of the money per stock, up to 8 stocks. Buy level RSI-2 25, sell at +10% or on the bounce, stop 25%. Options sell at +50% / −50%. Day trading and intraday signals are on; SPY trades 9:50 AM–2:00 PM.

The ACME trade in one line: Tuesday 11:20 AM, ACME has slid two days to $97 (RSI-2 = 18, below 25) → the bot buys 123 shares at $97.19 (≈$11,950) with a stop order at $72.89 → Wednesday it closes at $102.50, above its 5-day average → sold: +$653 (+5.5%). If it had jumped to $106.90 (+10%) first, it would have sold right then. The entries below each take one setting and show what changes in this trade.

1 · Connectionyour GitHub repo, broker keys and alerts

GitHub token

Stored in this browser only

A fine-grained personal access token that lets this page write the settings file, the keys and the pause file to your repo. Needs Contents, Secrets and Actions: Read and write on this one repo. Make it expire within 90 days and make a new one when it does.

Test connection tells you how fast GitHub answers, whether the token works, when it expires, and how many API calls are left this hour. Use it first whenever a save misbehaves.

Alpaca API key / secret

Encrypted GitHub secrets · paper keys

The paper-trading keys from your Alpaca dashboard. They are sealed in your browser with GitHub's public key and stored as encrypted secrets; this page never sees them again and they are never written to the repo. The bot reads them when a session starts.

Telegram alerts and commands

Two encrypted secrets (TELEGRAM_TOKEN, TELEGRAM_CHAT) · optional · recommended

Sends every alert — buys and sells with prices, stops, errors, the morning “Bot is up” message, the day summary and watchdog warnings — to your private Telegram chat. Set up: in Telegram open @BotFather, send /newbot, choose a name and a username ending in bot, copy the token; open your new bot and press Start; paste the token in Bot settings → 1 and press Connect Telegram. The page finds your chat, sends a test message and saves both values as secrets.

Commands (answered while the bot runs, about 9:20 AM–4:05 PM ET on trading days): /status running, market check, what it watches · /money money in trades, free to trade, today · /positions holdings with entry, now and exit levels · /watch buy / CALL / PUT candidates · /pause no new entries until /resume or the end of the session (open positions keep their stops and exits) · /resume · /help. Only your chat is answered; the bot never buys or sells on a chat command. Messages older than 30 minutes are ignored. This is the only alert channel (ntfy was removed on 2026-10-23).

Journal passphrase

Encrypted secret + saved in this browser · 12+ characters

Encrypts the bot's trade journal before it is published on the (public) site, and encrypts your synced settings. The Bot trades tab asks for the same passphrase on each device to unlock. Changing it means every device must enter the new one — and the old journal file can no longer be opened by the page.

Live API key / secret

Encrypted secrets · real money only

Leave empty for paper trading. Only used when section 7 is switched to real money; a paper key is rejected by the live endpoint, so the two can't be mixed up.

India bot (simulation)choose India with the switch at the top — that page shows only these settings · NSE · real prices, no orders

A separate bot for NSE stocks that runs after each NSE close (about 4:10 PM IST). It fills the buys it planned the evening before at that morning’s official opening price, sells at the close when an exit rule fires, and plans the next morning — exactly how the NSE test was run — in whole shares with 0.15% costs per side. No broker is connected. Choose India with the USA / India switch at the top of the page: Bot settings then shows only these settings (connect GitHub once from the USA side, section 1); Bot trades shows the India bot. Saved to bot/config-in.json with Save India settings.

2 · Risk profilehow much the bot may lose, per trade and in total

Preset: Conservative / Balanced / Aggressive

Default Balanced tested · yours: Aggressive

A preset fills in the seven risk numbers and the two sizing numbers below at once. Change any one of them and the preset shows “custom”.

Example. Balanced keeps at most 6 stocks × 10% = 60% of the money in stocks, so $40,000 always sits idle. Aggressive allows 8 × 12% = 96%. In the 5-year replay the same rules made +14% a year at Balanced and +23% a year at Aggressive, with a worst fall of −12% against −16%. “Aggressive” means more money working, not riskier trades.

Pick Conservative while learning or with real money; Aggressive once the practice account has behaved for a few months.

Risk per stock trade

Default 0.5% tested · yours: 1% · range 0.05–3%

The most one trade may lose if it hits its normal stop, as a % of your money. The bot works backwards from this to decide how many shares to buy: shares = (money × risk%) ÷ (distance to the stop).

Example. 1% of $100,000 = $1,000 at risk. ACME at $97.19 with a 3× range stop $4.20 below (the RSI-2 rule’s own stop distance): $1,000 ÷ $4.20 = 238 shares allowed by risk. But section 3 caps money per stock at 12% = $12,000 → $12,000 ÷ $97.19 = 123 shares. The smaller of the two wins, so you buy 123. A jumpier stock with a $9 stop distance would get $1,000 ÷ $9 = 111 shares — fewer shares, same dollars at risk.

Lower it (0.25–0.5%) with real money. Raising it above 1% rarely adds anything, because the 12% money cap usually decides anyway.

Risk per option trade

Default 0.5% · yours: 1% · range 0.05–3%

Same idea for CALLs and PUTs: the most an option trade may lose at its −50% stop.

Example. 1% = $1,000. A CALL at $3.50 ($350 a contract) loses $175 per contract at −50% → $1,000 ÷ $175 = 5 contracts allowed by risk ($1,750). Section 3 also caps CALLs at 3% of the money ($3,000) per trade, so 5 contracts it is.

Total risk cap

Default 3% · yours: 6% · range 0.5–15%

Adds up the risk-to-stop of everything open plus every waiting buy order. A new trade that would push the total over the cap is skipped (“total risk would exceed 6% of the account”).

Example. You hold 6 stocks each risking $1,000 = $6,000 = 6% of $100,000. The ACME buy would add $1,000 → 7%, over the cap → skipped, even though you have room for 8 stocks. Once one of the six sells, the next dip can be bought.

Max positions per sector

Default 2 · yours: 3 · range 1–10

How many stocks from one industry (Tech, Energy, Banks…) may be held at once. Dips often hit a whole sector on the same day; this stops you buying five oil stocks on one piece of oil news.

Example. You hold NVDA, AMD and MSFT (3 Tech). AAPL dips → “skipped — already 3 positions in Tech”. An Energy dip the same day is fine.

Daily loss limit

Default 3% · yours: 5% · range 0.5–20%

With the money split on (section 3, the default) this is the stock part’s daily limit, measured on the stock money. When the stock part is down this much on the day, no new stock buys until tomorrow; stocks already held keep their normal exits. CALLs, PUTs and SPY have their own limits in section 3 / 4c and carry on.

Example. Stock money $70,000 × 5% = $3,500. A bad morning: three held stocks fall and the stock part is −$3,600 by 11:00 → “stocks down $3,600 today — past the 5% limit on its $70,000 — no new stock trades today; CALLs and PUTs and SPY options carry on”. One Telegram alert.

With the split off, this is one account-wide limit and it also closes all options.

Weekly loss limit

Default 6% · yours: 10% · range 1–30%

If the whole account is down this much since Monday’s open, no new trades of any kind until next week. Open positions are managed to their exits as usual. This one covers all parts together.

Example. Monday open $100,000; by Wednesday $89,800 (−10.2%) → “Down 10.2% this week (limit 10%) — no new trades until next week”.

Losing streak limit

Default 3 · yours: 4 · range 1–10

This many losing trades in a row in one part (stocks, CALLs or PUTs — counted separately) ends that part’s day. A bad tape tends to stay bad.

Example. Four stock trades close at a loss one after another by 1:30 PM → no more stock buys today; a CALL that wins in between does not reset the stock count, and the CALL part keeps trading.

3 · Capital allocationevery money decision in one place: your stake, and the shares for stocks, CALLs, PUTs and the SPY sleeve

Money the bot works with (your stake)

Default empty = the whole account · yours: empty → $100,000 · range $0–$100,000,000

Enter the amount you actually intend to trade with. Every size, every loss limit and the pattern-day-trader rule are then computed on that figure instead of the practice account’s $100,000, and the Bot trades tab shows profit and loss against it.

Example. Stake $20,000 → the stock part is 70% = $14,000, a stock gets 12% = $1,680 (17 shares of ACME), the daily stock limit is 5% = $700. Everything scales down together, so the practice run behaves exactly like the money you mean to use.

Set it before you judge the practice results — otherwise the bot is sized for $100,000 you may not have.

Trade stocks / Trade CALL options / Trade PUT options

Default on / on / off · yours: on / on / on · PUTs experimental

Switch each instrument on or off. Stocks are the researched core. CALLs ride the same dips with options (limited loss, more leverage). PUTs are the mirror image — bought when a stock in a downtrend spikes up — and were never backtested.

Example. On the ACME dip the bot buys the shares (stocks on) and 5 CALL contracts (CALLs on). If a downtrend stock pops (RSI-2 above 75 with your level), PUTs on would buy a PUT on it. In the 5-year stand-in test, the PUT side lost money (−5% a year), which is why its share is small.

Where the money goes (the four shares)

Default stocks 55% · CALLs 30% · PUTs 10% · SPY 5% · yours: stocks 70% · CALLs 20% · PUTs 5% · SPY 5%

All four shares of your stake sit together here with a bar showing the split. You set the CALL, PUT and SPY shares; stocks always get the rest. Each part can never use more than its share, whatever the others do, and nothing is ever sold to rebalance — a full part simply skips new trades until money comes back. If the three add up to more than 100% the page shows an error and Save is disabled until they fit.

Example. $100,000 → stocks $70,000, CALLs $20,000, PUTs $5,000, SPY $5,000. Six stocks at ≈$12,000 each = $72,000 — more than $70,000 — so the sixth stock buy is cut to the $70,000 − $60,000 = $10,000 left (103 shares of ACME instead of 123), and a seventh is skipped (“stock money $70,000, $70,000 already in use”) even though 8 positions are allowed. Meanwhile a CALL can still be bought: its $20,000 is separate.

Prefer stocks: they have 33 years of evidence; options and SPY are the experiments. If a part keeps hitting “money … already in use”, give it more; if it keeps losing, give it less or switch it off.

Separate daily loss limits for stocks, CALLs and PUTs

Default on

On: each part has its own daily loss limit (stocks: section 2’s limit on the stock money; CALLs and PUTs: the options limit below on their own money) and its own losing-streak count, so a bad day in one never stops the others. Off: one shared pool and one account-wide daily limit; the shares still cap each part.

Example. CALLs lose $2,100 by noon (over 10% of $20,000) → CALLs stop and the open CALL is closed; the ACME stock buy at 1:00 PM still goes ahead. With the switch off, the same loss is only 2.1% of the account — under the 5% daily limit — so nothing stops at all.

CALLs share / PUTs share

Default 30 / 10% · yours: 20 / 5% · range 0–100% each

The part of your stake each option type may use together (see the four shares above).

Example. CALLs 20% = $20,000. Each CALL trade is also capped at 3% per trade ($3,000, below), so up to six or seven CALLs can be open before the share is used up.

SPY quick-options share

Default 5% · yours: 5% · range 0–50%

The money the SPY sleeve (section 4c) may use. One SPY trade spends up to all of it. Switch the sleeve off in 4c and this share goes back to stocks.

Example. 5% = $5,000. A SPY CALL quoted $2.05 ($205 a contract) → $5,000 ÷ $205 = 24 contracts. At the +15% take-profit that is +$738; at the −15% stop −$738.

SPY money per trade

Default 100% · range 10–100% · yours: 100%

How much of the SPY share one SPY trade may use. Trades still run one at a time; the rest of the share simply waits. Lower it to make each SPY trade — and each SPY loss — smaller.

Example. SPY share 5% = $5,000; contracts at $2.05 ($205 each). 100%: 24 contracts ($4,920); a −15% stop costs about $744 (0.74% of the account). 50%: $2,500 → 12 contracts ($2,460); the same stop costs about $372. 20%: $1,000 → 4 contracts; about $124. The daily loss limit (25% of $5,000 = $1,250) and “2 losses in a row” still apply, so at 50% two losing trades cost about $744 instead of $1,488.

Options daily loss limit

Default 10% · yours: 10% · range 1–50%

If CALLs lose this % of the CALL money in a day, new CALLs stop and open CALLs are closed; the same for PUTs on the PUT money.

Example. CALL money $20,000 × 10% = $2,000; PUT money $5,000 × 10% = $500. Two losing PUTs of $300 each → PUTs stop for the day; CALLs and stocks carry on.

Size it in: % of account / fixed dollars

Per instrument · default % of account

Choose whether the money limits for that instrument are read as a percentage of the current money or as fixed dollar amounts. The pair you don’t pick is dimmed and ignored.

Example. Stocks in dollars, $5,000 per trade: ACME gets $5,000 ÷ $97.19 = 51 shares whatever the account size.

Per trade — stocks / CALLs / PUTs

Default 10% / 2% / 1% tested (stocks) · yours: 12% / 3% / 2% · range 1–30% / 0.5–10% / 0.5–10%

The most one trade may spend. The actual size is the smaller of this and what the risk rule (section 2) allows.

Example. Stocks 12% of $100,000 = $12,000 → 123 shares of ACME at $97.19. CALLs 3% = $3,000 → 8 contracts at $350, but the 1% risk rule allows only 5 → 5 contracts. PUTs 2% = $2,000.

All together — stocks / CALLs / PUTs (ceilings)

Default 0 = no ceiling · range 0–100% or dollars

An optional hard ceiling on everything that instrument has open plus waiting orders. 0 means only the position counts and the shares above limit it. Since the shares already do this job, most people leave these at 0.

Example. CALL ceiling $4,000: with $3,500 of CALLs open, a new $1,750 CALL is skipped (“call money ceiling $4,000, $3,500 already in use”).

Max stock positions / Max option positions

Default 6 / 4 tested (stocks) · yours: 8 / 6 · range 1–20 / 0–10

How many may be held at once. Option positions count CALLs and PUTs together.

Example. You hold 8 stocks; ACME dips → “no slot”. Position count is checked before money, so even with money left the ninth stock waits.

4 · Strategythe tested values are the defaults — change with care

Which dips to buy first

Default Leaders first · or Deepest dip first

Decides the order when more stocks dip on the same day than the bot has room for. Leaders first buys the dips whose 3-month return beat the market (S&P 500) most; strong stocks that stumble bounce back fastest. In the 33-year research this beat the original order in every period, with the same 70% win rate and a slightly smaller worst fall. Deepest dip first is the order used in the original test.

Example. A sell-off day: ACME (+8% vs the market over 3 months, RSI-2 18), BETA (−4% vs market, RSI-2 5) and GAMA (+2%, RSI-2 12) all dip, with room for two. Leaders first buys ACME and GAMA; deepest-dip would have bought BETA and GAMA.

Intraday signals

Default off · yours: on experimental

Off: the bot uses yesterday’s closing prices to decide, so a stock that dips during today is bought tomorrow morning at the open — exactly what the research tested. On: the live price counts as today’s close, so the bot can buy during the day as soon as RSI-2 crosses the level. More trades, earlier fills, never tested on history.

Example. ACME closed Monday at $100 (RSI-2 40). Tuesday 11:20 it is $97 and RSI-2 on the live price is 18. On: bought at 11:20 for $97.19. Off: nothing happens Tuesday; if ACME closes at $97, it is bought Wednesday at the open, perhaps $98.

RSI-2 buy level

Default 10 tested · yours: 25 · range 2–30

RSI-2 is a 0–100 gauge of how hard a stock fell over the last 2 days: 100 × up-moves ÷ (up-moves + down-moves). Two down days = 0. The bot buys a stock above its 200-day average when RSI-2 drops below this level. Lower = rarer, sharper dips; higher = more trades on milder dips.

Example. ACME: Friday $105 (+$1), Monday $100 (−$5) → up 1, down 5 → RSI-2 = 100 × 1 ÷ 6 = 17. Below 25 → buy. Below 10? No — at the tested level this dip would be skipped and the bot would wait for a deeper one. Monday $100 then Tuesday $97 (−$3): two down days → RSI-2 = 0 → buy at either level. In the 5-year replay your 25 made +27.7% a year against +22.9% for 10, with the same worst fall, because it finds more trades.

Max hold

Default 10 sessions tested · range 1–30

A stock that has not bounced by then is sold at the close anyway; a dip that lasts two weeks was not a dip.

Example. ACME bought Tuesday at $97.19 drifts sideways around $96 for two weeks, never closing above its 5-day average. On the 10th trading day it is sold at the close for about −1%.

Stock take-profit

Default +10% · yours: +10% · range 0–100% (0 = off)

A stock up this much from its buy price is sold at once, any time of day, instead of waiting for the RSI-2 exit at the close. On the day it was bought it sells only when the pattern-day-trader rule allows a same-day sale. The bounce exit and the 10-day maximum still apply, whichever comes first. The research’s best stock rule (+28% a year over 33 years vs +28.3% without it, with a smaller worst fall).

Example. ACME bought at $97.19. Wednesday 10:40 AM good news lifts it to $107.00 (+10.1%) → sold right then, +$1,206. Without this setting the bot would have waited for the close and taken whatever price ACME ended the day at.

Stock stop (hard floor)

Default 25% · yours: 25% · range 2–60%

A stock down this much from its buy price is sold. The stop travels with every buy as an order at the broker, so it works even if the bot is down. It is a disaster brake, not the normal exit. The research tried a tight 5% stop: it cut the yearly gain almost in half, because dips often go a little lower before they bounce and a tight stop sells at the low. When you tighten it, the bot moves the stops of stocks it already holds up to the new level.

Example. ACME bought at $97.19 → stop order at $72.89. A crash through $72.89 sells automatically at the broker, −$2,989 at most. At 5% the stop would be $92.33: in the real trade ACME touched $96 on Tuesday afternoon before bouncing — a 5% stop would have survived that one, but on many trades it would not.

Option take-profit / Option stop

Default +50% / −50% · yours: +50% / −50% · ranges 5–300% / 5–90%

On the option’s own price. The take-profit rests at the broker as a limit order; the stop is watched by the bot. Options also leave 3 days before expiry and on Fed-decision afternoons, and are held one night at most. With a tight stop, a new option is only bought when its bid/ask spread is under half the stop (never over 20%), because a wide spread would trigger the stop on its own.

Example. 5 ACME CALLs at $3.50 → take-profit order at $5.25 (+$875 if hit), stop at $1.75 (−$875). A $3.42/$3.58 quote has a 4.5% spread: fine for a 50% stop; it would be refused for a stop under 9%.

Cancel unfilled entries after

Default 45 min · range 5–240

A buy order that has not filled by then is cancelled: the setup is stale.

Example. ACME’s buy is placed at $97.19 at 11:20 but the price jumps to $98.50 and never comes back. At 12:05 the order is cancelled; if ACME dips again later, a fresh order is placed at the new price.

Entry window closes at

Default 11:00 AM · yours: 3:30 PM · range 10:00 AM – 3:45 PM ET

The last time of day the swing part may open a new stock or option trade (entries start at 9:45, or 10:15 on CPI / jobs-report mornings). Later dips wait for tomorrow. Open positions are always managed, whatever the time.

Example. With 3:30 PM, ACME’s 11:20 dip and a second stock’s 2:50 PM dip are both bought. With the default 11:00, only ACME; the 2:50 dip is bought the next morning if it still qualifies.

4b · Day tradingin and out the same day — experimental

Day-trade (on/off)

Default off · yours: on

A separate engine: opening-range breakouts and VWAP pullbacks on 5-minute bars, in and out the same day, flat by 3:50 PM. Rules in Day-trading rules. The researched version made +0.5% a year — small — which is why it is off by default and sized small. Under $25,000 of money the pattern-day-trader rule allows only 3 same-day round trips in 5 business days; the bot counts them and stops.

Example. 9:45: ACME’s first 15 minutes ranged $100.20–$101.00. 9:52 it trades $101.55 on heavy volume with the market (SPY) above its VWAP → day-trade buy at $101.65, stop $100.40, target $104.15 (2× the risk), both resting at the broker. 11:10 it hits $104.15 → +$245 on 98 shares. If it had stalled, the 60-minute time stop would have closed it near breakeven.

Day-trade stocks / CALLs / PUTs

Default on / off / on · yours: on / on / on

A long setup is taken as shares (or a CALL if that is on); a short setup is taken as a PUT (never by shorting stock).

Opening range

Default 15 min tested · range 5–60

The first minutes after 9:30 that define the day’s range; a break out of it is the signal.

Example. 15 → the 9:30–9:45 high/low. 30 → 9:30–10:00: fewer, later, more reliable breaks.

Max day trades per day · Losses in a row that end the day

Default 3 · 2

Example. Two day trades lose before 10:30 → done for the day, whatever the setups later.

Risk per day trade

Default 0.5% · range 0.1–2%

Example. 0.5% of $100,000 = $500 at the stop. ACME entry $101.65, stop $100.40 → $1.25 a share → $500 ÷ $1.25 = 400 shares by risk, but the stock money-per-trade cap ($12,000 → 118 shares) decides: 118 shares.

Target

Default 2 R · range 1–4

In multiples of the risk. 2 = aim for twice the distance to the stop.

Example. Risk $1.25 → target $101.65 + $2.50 = $104.15. At 3 R: $105.40 — hit less often, pays more.

Minimum relative volume

Default 1.2 · range 0.5–3

Today’s volume so far against a normal day. 1.2 = 20% busier than usual; a breakout without volume is skipped.

Time stop

Default 60 min · range 15–180

A day trade that has not reached 0.3 R in profit after this long is closed.

Option take-profit / stop · Option expiry, max days out

Default +50% / −40% · 7 days

For day-traded CALLs and PUTs, on the option’s own price; the stock-level stop and target apply too. Never same-day expiry.

Entries from / until

Default 9:45 AM – 3:30 PM · ranges 9:35–12:00 / 10:00–3:45 ET

No new day trades outside this window; everything is flat by 3:50 PM.

4c · SPY quick optionsCALLs and PUTs on SPY, minutes at a time — experimental

SPY quick options (on/off)

Default on · yours: on · not backtested

A separate part of the bot that trades only SPY options, holding each for minutes. It spends only its own share of the stake (section 3) and has its own brakes, so it can never touch the money of the stock, CALL or PUT parts. These rules have not been tested on history — minute-by-minute options prices are a paid dataset — so judge it on the practice account before trusting it. Switched off, any open trade is still managed to its exit.

How a trade goes. 10:04:12 SPY trades at $502.60, above its day’s VWAP ($501.90) and through the high of the last 15 minutes ($502.50) → within a second the bot buys 24 next-day SPY 503 CALLs at $2.05 ($4,920 of the $5,000 share) and rests a sell order at $2.36 (+15%). 10:07:40 SPY keeps rising, the CALL touches $2.36 → sold by the broker: +$744 in 3½ minutes. If instead SPY stalled: at 10:14 the 10-minute time stop sells at market, perhaps $2.08 (+$72) or $1.95 (−$240). If SPY reversed hard: the first quote at or under $1.74 (−15%) sells at market, about −$744.

How it decides

Direction from SPY against its day’s VWAP (volume-weighted average price): above → CALLs only, below → PUTs only, sitting on it → nothing. It buys when SPY breaks the high (CALL) or low (PUT) of the last 15 minutes and the last 5-minute bar closed that way on at least average volume. The contract is nearest the money, expiring 1 trading day out (never same-day), bid/ask spread under 5%. One trade at a time. Entries fire from the live SPY price tick; exits react to the option’s own live quotes (both well under a second), with a 1-second check as the fallback.

SPY take-profit / SPY stop

Default +15% / −15% · ranges 5–100% / 5–60%

On the option’s own price. Same-size wins and losses with a 10-minute hold is a fast, coin-flip-like trade; any edge has to come from the direction filter — which is exactly what the practice account will show.

Example. Bought at $2.05 → sell order at $2.36; stop at $1.74. 24 contracts → ±$744.

SPY time stop

Default 10 min · range 2–120

Out after this many minutes whatever the price.

SPY max trades per day · SPY losses in a row · SPY daily loss limit

Default 6 · 2 · 25% · ranges 1–20 · 1–6 · 5–100%

The sleeve’s own brakes: a daily maximum, a losing-streak stop, and a loss limit as a % of its own money. Any of them ends its day; the other parts carry on. Telegram says which.

Example. Two losses of $744 = $1,488 = 30% of the $5,000 share → both the streak rule and the 25% limit ($1,250) end its day at the second loss. Nothing else in the bot changes.

SPY expiry, max days out

Default 1 · range 1–5 trading days

1 = the next trading day (Friday’s trade buys Monday’s expiry). Same-day (0DTE) options are never bought: they lose value fastest.

Trades from / until

Default 9:50 AM – 3:30 PM · yours: 9:50 AM – 2:00 PM · ranges 9:35–12:00 / 10:00–3:30 ET

Its own hours, separate from the other parts. No entries before the first 15 minutes of bars exist or after the end time; open trades are flat by 3:45 PM.

5 · Guardswhat makes the bot stand aside

Economic calendar guard

Default on

On Fed-decision days the bot takes no new trades from 1:30 PM and closes options before the 2:00 PM announcement; on CPI and jobs-report mornings entries start at 10:15 instead of 9:45; Fed-minutes afternoons are treated like Fed days for options. The calendar is in the repo and refreshed by the research runs.

Example. Wednesday is a Fed day. ACME dips at 1:45 PM → skipped (“Fed decision in minutes”). The ACME CALL still open from Tuesday is sold at 1:30 PM rather than risk the 2:00 PM swing.

Breaking-news guard

Default on

Market-wide headlines about war, policy shocks or a crisis pause new trades; a stock with its own fresh bad news (earnings miss, lawsuit, downgrade) is skipped that day. Uses Alpaca’s news feed, refreshed every minute.

Example. ACME dips because its CEO resigned this morning → “ACME skipped — fresh news: CEO steps down”. A dip caused by the whole market falling is fine: that is the kind the strategy wants.

Telegram alerts · Include prices and profit in alerts

Default on · on

Every buy and sell, every halt, errors, the morning “Bot is up” and the 4 PM summary go to your private Telegram chat (set up in section 1). With detail on, a buy shows size and price and a sell shows entry, exit and profit.

6 · Stocks it watchesthe universe

Scan the whole market / Use my list

Default my list (30 large stocks) · yours: scan

Scan: every morning the bot ranks every tradable US stock by trading volume and watches the most-traded ones (below). List: only the tickers you type. The research used a fixed list of 39 large stocks; the scan was not tested but finds the same kind of liquid names plus more of them.

Example. With the scan at 200, ACME is watched only if it is among the 200 busiest stocks that morning; a thinly traded small company never is, however sharp its dip.

How many stocks to watch

Default 200 · range 50–400

More stocks = more dips to choose from (leaders first then matters more) and a longer check each beat.

Minimum share price

Default $5 · range $1–500

Skips penny stocks, which dip for reasons the strategy cannot read.

My list

Up to 60 tickers · used when “Use my list” is chosen

US symbols in capitals; a class letter uses a dot (BRK.B, not BRK-B). The page refuses a list with a ticker the bot could not use, an empty list, or more than 60.

7 · Modepaper or real money

Practice (paper) / Real money (live)

Default paper · yours: paper

Paper uses Alpaca’s practice account with real prices and no real money. Switching to live needs the live keys in section 1, the phrase I UNDERSTAND THE RISK typed exactly, and the go-live checklist on the page (practice record, limits, experimental parts small or off — including SPY quick options). A paper key is rejected by the live endpoint, so the two cannot be mixed up.

Live size

Default 25% · range 5–100%

With real money, every budget and risk figure is scaled by this: start small and raise it as the live record builds.

Example. 25% → the ACME buy would be 31 shares instead of 123, risking $250 instead of $1,000.

Creates bot/STOP

This is not a gentle pause. It cancels every open order and sells every position at market within seconds, then keeps the bot idle until you press Resume. For “no new trades but keep what I hold”, use /pause in Telegram instead.

The next check starts trading again under the current settings.

Words used hereplain meanings

RSI-2 — a 0–100 gauge of the last two days’ move; low = a sharp drop. 200-day average — the average closing price of the last 200 trading days; above it = long-term uptrend. 5-day average — the bounce exit level. Stop — a price at which a position is sold to cap the loss. Take-profit — a price at which a winning position is sold. Risk (R) — the distance from entry to stop; “2 R” = twice that. VWAP — today’s average price weighted by volume; price above it means buyers are in charge. Spread — the gap between the price you can sell at (bid) and buy at (ask). Share (of the stake) — the slice of your money a part of the bot may use. Contract — one option, which covers 100 shares, so a $2.05 option costs $205. DTE — days to expiry; 0DTE = expires today. Pattern-day-trader rule — under $25,000, only 3 same-day round trips in 5 business days. Paper — the practice account.
