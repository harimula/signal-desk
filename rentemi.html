The 30-year record behind the auto values
House prices: 9.3% a year (auto), about 6% over 20 years, 3.6% now
A Centre for Social and Economic Progress paper puts house price growth at 9.3% a year from 1991 to 2021 and 3.7% a year from 2017 to 2022. CRISIL, as quoted by NoBroker, gives about 6% a year over 20 years. The RBI house price index rose 3.6% in the year to Q1 FY27. Sources: CSEP, NoBroker, RBI index via Devdiscourse.
Investments: 12% a year (auto), from the Nifty 50's own 30 years
The Nifty 50 started on 3 November 1995 at 1,000 and closed near 22,620 on 14 October 2026. That is 10.6% a year in price over 31 years (my arithmetic, in line with Finnovate's 10.59% to March 2026). NSE's factsheet shows a dividend yield of 1.35% (May 2026), and it has mostly stayed between 1% and 2%. Price plus dividends comes to about 12%. Shorter windows are weaker. A Nifty 50 index fund returned 11.4% a year over 10 years, 10.0% over 20 years, 5.7% over 5 years and −8.8% over the last year, after fund costs, to 16 October 2026. Sources: Finnovate, Kotak Neo, Bajaj AMC, Integrato.
Rent: 6% a year (auto, a proxy)
I found no clean 30-year rent series. The auto value follows average CPI inflation of about 5.8% from 2000 to 2026. Urban housing CPI ran between 3.2% and 4.5% in 2020 to 2022, and some areas saw rents rise about 10% a year from 2019 to 2024. Sources: Rupaywise, Business Standard, Crib.
Loan rate: your 9.075% stays
Kotak puts the 10-year average home loan rate near 9.2%, with a low of 6.7% in 2020 to 2022. Source: Kotak.

These are rounded figures from secondary sources covering different periods. They are not an official series, and past returns do not predict the next 20 years.

How this is calculated
  • Buy: you pay the down payment, the EMI for the loan tenure, and property tax, society and upkeep that rise every year. At the end you own the house at its grown price, minus any loan left.
  • Rent: rent steps up once a year. Down payment minus upfront costs is invested on day one. Each month, the cost of buying minus the rent goes into investments. When rent passes the cost of buying, the shortfall comes out of the investments.
  • Investment return and house price growth are yearly rates, applied monthly. Net worth is the house value minus loan, or the investment balance, at the end of your stay.
  • Tax: with the old-regime box ticked, buying saves the slab rate on principal repaid (up to ₹1.5 L) plus interest paid (up to ₹2 L) each year, as on your Tax Benefits tab. Renting saves the HRA amount you enter. Both savings are invested.
  • Different from the sheet: the sheet scores renting as investments minus rent paid and buying as house value minus all costs, which are not on the same footing. This version compares what each path is worth at the end, with identical cash going out.
  • Growth assumptions: "30-year history" sets rent growth, house price growth and investment return from the record listed above. "Last 5 years" and "Your sheet" are the other two sets. Edit any of the three fields to use your own.
  • Auto-fill from the house cost, using your sheet's ratios: down payment 20%, rent 0.3% a month, upfront rent costs of 4 months' rent, property tax and society maintenance 0.5% a year each, upkeep 0.1% a year. At ₹1 Cr these give exactly your sheet's figures.
  • Left out:
    • selling costs, capital gains tax, loan prepayment, home insurance and vacancy. The rent deposit is treated as spent, as in the sheet.
